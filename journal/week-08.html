What I discovered from Sydney's climate

The question

For my selected site in Sydney, I investigated how visible digital and electricity infrastructure represents the actual infrastructure supporting the city. I wanted to understand whether mapped telecommunications facilities and power infrastructure could explain the site's connectivity and its relationship with the wider digital network. My question focuses on network, scale, and place: what can open geographical data reveal, and what remains invisible?

The data

I used three sources, accessed on 4 November 2026. OpenStreetMap (OSM) provides geographical data under the Open Database License (ODbL). OSM-derived maps can be publicly displayed with attribution, while distributed derivative databases must comply with ODbL share-alike requirements. TeleGeography's Submarine Cable Map allows screenshots to be reproduced under CC BY-SA 4.0 with credit, but its underlying database cannot be freely republished. Data Center Map lists 122 data centres in Sydney. Its Terms of Use restrict copying, scraping, redistribution, and database integration without permission. I used it for reference rather than copying its data.

Write the proxy sentence here, once per indicator: I am using average monthly temperature at the Sydney Airport station to stand for Sydney's seasonal temperature pattern, and it might fail because one station cannot capture temperature differences across the entire city. I am using average monthly precipitation at the Sydney Airport station to stand for Sydney's seasonal rainfall pattern, and it might fail because rainfall can vary across locations within the city.

The method

I used two Overpass Turbo queries: [out:json][timeout:60]; nwr["telecom"]({{bbox}}); out center; [out:json][timeout:60]; ( nwr["power"="substation"]({{bbox}}); nwr["power"="tower"]({{bbox}}); ); out center; The predicates identify objects with the specified tags, while {{bbox}} limits the search to the visible map extent. My extent covered central Sydney, the North Shore, and surrounding eastern and southern urban areas shown in the map viewport. I used the default Overpass Turbo server, https://overpass-api.de/api/, without changing the server settings

What I found

The comparison reveals that different databases produce different pictures of the same urban environment. OSM records many electricity-related objects, but its telecommunications records are fewer. The commercial inventory reports 122 data centres, demonstrating that the number of mapped objects depends heavily on each database's purpose, classification system, and contributors.

What this does not show

I am using mapped power infrastructure to stand for the electricity network my site could connect to, and it might fail because OSM records depend on contributors identifying and tagging substations and towers, while unmapped or differently classified grid components are excluded from these predicates. Therefore, the query cannot establish the complete availability or capacity of electricity at my site.

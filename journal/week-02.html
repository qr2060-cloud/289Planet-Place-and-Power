The question

How does Sydney's seasonal climate pattern, shown through its climate normal and station network, compare with broader regional patterns in Australia?

The data

I used Esri's World Historical Climate – Monthly Averages for GHCN-D Stations for 1981–2010, a global climate layer based on NOAA NCEI's Global Historical Climatology Network-Daily (GHCN-Daily), Version 3.24. The dataset contains monthly mean temperature and precipitation data for the 1981–2010 period. Esri is the publisher, while the underlying station and climate data come from NOAA NCEI's GHCN-Daily dataset. The layer is licensed under the Esri Master License Agreement. The attribute table contains 7,480 records, although the older layer description refers to a smaller station count; this discrepancy reflects the layer's revision history and is an important data-provenance issue.

The method

I first examined the global station network in ArcGIS Online and then filtered the GHCN-D ID field to identify stations in Australia using the country's GHCN-D prefix. I then selected the Sydney Airport station and applied a second filter using its specific GHCN-D station ID, with the two filter expressions joined by AND, so that only the selected station remained visible. For the temperature chart, I used the twelve monthly Avg. Temp C fields from January through December and calculated the Mean. I repeated the process using the twelve monthly Mean Prcp mm fields to produce the precipitation chart. Both charts use the 1981–2010 climate normal.

What I found

Sydney has a mild coastal climate, with warm summers, relatively mild winters, and precipitation throughout the year without a pronounced dry season. Rainfall is somewhat higher during the warmer part of the year, particularly in February and April, although substantial precipitation also occurs during winter. Compared with Australia's hotter and much drier interior, Sydney is more representative of southeastern coastal Australia than of the country as a whole. At the global scale, the broader pattern of wetter tropical regions and drier subtropical regions can be connected to atmospheric circulation and the movement of rising and descending air.

What this does not show

The analysis cannot show that one Sydney station represents the climate of the entire city or Australia. Local conditions can vary with elevation, distance from the coast, and urban geography. The 1981–2010 baseline is also older than the current 1991–2020 WMO normal, so these results should not be interpreted as a description of today's climate. Finally, the uneven distribution of stations means that areas with few or no observations cannot automatically be interpreted as climatically uniform or lacking climate patterns; the map also reflects the history of human measurement.
